## Positioning

A closed-loop trading system that watches everything you do, finds what actually makes money, kills what doesn't, and re-tests itself continuously so it never confuses luck with edge. It sits on top of a live feed so it can also grade *how* you got filled, not just what you traded.

## The core principle

Most trading apps give you either **data** (charts, feeds) or **journaling** (a notebook). Neither closes the loop. Foundry's whole identity is one sentence: **every insight must survive out-of-sample testing before it's allowed to change a rule.**

That's the difference between an app that says "you win 70% on Mondays" and one that says "you win 70% on Mondays, and that held up on the last 6 months you never tuned on, so size it up."

## Architecture

The diagram above is the skeleton — eight layers, with the feedback arrow being the actual product. Walking it:

**Layer 1 — Ingestion.** Broker API sync (pulls fills, fees, timestamps automatically), CSV/manual import as fallback, and a real-time market data feed over WebSocket carrying Level 1 and Level 2. The feed isn't decoration — it's what lets Layer 4 compute your slippage.

**Layer 2 — Storage.** Trade ledger, tick store, feature store. The feature store is the piece most people skip and it's the one that matters: every trade gets reduced to ~40 numeric features (time of day, spread at entry, ATR, distance from VWAP, regime label, size vs. your average, etc.) so patterns can be mined statistically instead of eyeballed.

**Layer 3 — Enrichment.** Auto-tagging: the model reads each trade and assigns a setup label, which you can correct. Regime labeling tags each trade with market state (trending/choppy, high/low volatility). This is where raw trades become analyzable.

**Layer 4 — Analytics.** Expectancy per tag, R-multiple distribution, profit factor, plus **execution quality** — your fill price vs. the NBBO at order time, in basis points. That last one is the module nobody builds and it's often worth more than any strategy tweak.

**Layer 5 — ML core.** Pattern mining across the feature store, a win-probability model, and the **overfitting guard** (more on this below).

**Layer 6 — Strategy lab.** Backtesting, walk-forward optimization, out-of-sample validation, paper trading.

**Layer 7 — AI advisor.** An LLM with tool access to the feature store. It doesn't guess — it queries the stats, cites sample sizes, and proposes specific rule changes.

**Layer 8 — UI.** Dashboard, alerts, coaching feed.

## Beyond what you asked for

The "and more" that actually earns its place:

- **Execution analytics.** Slippage, fill quality, and whether your entries degrade during high-vol windows. Ties straight into the live feed.
- **Behavioral module.** Detects revenge trading (re-entry within X minutes of a loss), tilt (size escalation after losses), time-of-day decay, and overtrading. Often finds 3–5x more edge than strategy work.
- **Risk engine.** Max drawdown, Kelly-fraction position sizing suggestion, correlation between your open setups so you're not accidentally 4x long the same thing.
- **Regime-aware edge.** The same setup graded separately in trending vs. choppy markets, so you learn *when* to run it, not just *whether*.
- **Alerts.** "You're about to take your worst-performing setup in your worst-performing regime" — pushed live, before entry.

## The anti-overfit discipline

This is the make-or-break. Four hard rules for ai

1. **Minimum sample gate.** No setup gets a verdict under ~100 trades. Anything less is labeled "insufficient data."
2. **Walk-forward, not backtest.** Optimize on a window, test on the *next* window, roll forward. Repeat.
3. **Holdout set.** Reserve the most recent slice of data and never let the tuner touch it.
4. **Multiple-testing correction.** If you test 200 patterns, some will look great by chance. The guard penalizes for how many hypotheses you burned.

The **overfit score** in the mockup is a single number derived from how much of your backtest edge survived the out-of-sample test. High score = fragile. Low = real.

## UX principles

- **One verdict per setup**: keep, size up, cut, or "not enough data." No walls of numbers.
- **Every claim shows its sample size and confidence.** No naked percentages.
- **The AI proposes, you approve.** Nothing changes your rules automatically without a click.
- **Latency and feed status always visible** so you know if you're on live or delayed data.
- **The journal is the input.** If tagging is a chore, the whole system starves — so tagging has to be one tap, auto-suggested, correctable.

## Tech stack

- Feed: WebSocket stream, direct exchange feeds where cost allows, consolidated otherwise
- Store: time-series DB for ticks, relational for ledger, vector/columnar for features
- ML: gradient-boosted trees for the win-probability model (interpretable, handles tabular features well), not a black-box neural net — you need to explain *why*
- Advisor: LLM with function-calling into the stats layer
- Mobile-first, because you tag trades on your phone

## Build order

**MVP :** broker sync → auto-tagging → expectancy per tag → dashboard. This alone beats 90% of journals.
**V2:** execution analytics + behavioral module + regime tagging.
**V3:** strategy lab (backtest + walk-forward + holdout).
**V4:** live AI coach with pre-trade alerts.
**V5:** paper-trade deployment of AI-proposed rules.


Don't let the AI "continuously improve" by retuning constantly. Retune on a schedule (weekly), not on every new trade — otherwise you're chasing noise. The loop should be slow and deliberate, not twitchy. Constant improvement done wrong is just curve-fitting in real time.
