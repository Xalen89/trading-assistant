CONFLUENCE REQUIREMENTS

Definition
Every strategy must be defined as three parts, not one:
  (1) trigger condition
  (2) a named set of confluence conditions, each with an explicit weight
      and an explicit data source
  (3) a minimum confluence gate that must be met before the trade is valid

Structural rules
- No strategy may fire on the trigger alone. The gate is mandatory.
- Every confluence condition must be falsifiable and sourced from real,
  point-in-time data. No vague conditions ("looks strong", "good momentum").
- Weights are configuration, not hardcoded. They must be tunable and must
  be validated out-of-sample like any other parameter.
- Condition weights must be independent enough to be informative. Flag any
  two conditions that are >0.9 correlated, since they double-count.

Runtime behaviour
- Before entry, render the live confluence checklist: each condition with
  met / not met / pending status and its data source visible.
- Show the running confluence score against the strategy's gate.
- Alert when the trigger fires but confluence is below the gate.
- On every trade taken, log the confluence score AND the per-condition
  state at entry time. This is non-negotiable — no logging, no learning.

Analytics requirements
- Measure win rate, expectancy, profit factor, and max adverse excursion
  broken down by confluence score bucket.
- Measure win rate and expectancy broken down by EACH individual
  confluence condition (present vs absent).
- Rank conditions by measured contribution to expectancy.
- Flag conditions that add no measurable edge, with sample size shown.
- Flag conditions that are inversely correlated with wins.
- Report the optimal gate threshold and how much it moves expectancy.

AI advisor requirements
- The AI must explain which confluences correlated with wins, always
  citing sample size and out-of-sample confirmation status.
- The AI must never state an edge for a condition with < 100 samples
  without labelling it unconfirmed.
- When proposing a rule change, the AI must state which condition it is
  adding, removing, or reweighting, and the measured effect.

Validation
- The whole confluence model for a strategy must pass walk-forward with a
  holdout set before being promoted from paper to live.
- A condition that fails out-of-sample must be demoted to "watch" rather
  than silently kept.
Your Confluence Requirements spec is exceptionally rigorous—it reads like a professional-grade quant fund's risk and validation playbook. It completely solves the "journaling vs. data" problem by forcing objective quantification and strict out-of-sample discipline.
While the spec is already solid enough to hand directly to an AI or developer, four minor technical blind spots can be added to bulletproof it against runtime edge-cases, data leaks, and UI friction.
Recommended Additions & Improvements
1. Weight Normalization (UX & Logic)
* The Gap: If weights are arbitrary numbers (e.g., Condition A = 3, Condition B = 2, Gate = 4), the scoring math becomes hard to interpret quickly on a live mobile screen.
* The Fix: Require that all condition weights for a strategy normalize to a 0–100% scale (or sum to a fixed base like 10.0). This makes the live confluence score instantly readable as a percentage (e.g., "78% Confluence Met").
2. Stale Data & Latency Fallback Handling
* The Gap: In live streaming, what happens if a specific data source (e.g., an L2 order book feed or volume profile API) lags, drops, or loses connection right when a trigger fires?
* The Fix: Add a runtime fallback rule: "If any data source feeding an active confluence condition has a latency greater than X milliseconds or loses connection, the condition defaults to 'Pending / Stale', and the trade is blocked from auto-executing until resolved."
3. Look-Ahead Bias and Repainting Protection
* The Gap: The spec mentions "point-in-time data," but in backtesting, indicators like MACD or moving averages can sometimes use future bar closes or repaint if not strictly isolated.
* The Fix: Explicitly mandate that historical feature calculation use strictly closed-bar data (e.g., indicator values evaluated at the close of candle t-1 for execution at the open of candle t) to prevent look-ahead bias during walk-forward optimization.
4. Regime Interaction Layer
* The Gap: A confluence condition might have a high win-rate contribution overall, but perform terribly during specific market states (e.g., a momentum confluence failing entirely in a choppy consolidation range).
* The Fix: Require that individual condition analytics break down performance by market regime (Trending vs. Choppy, High vs. Low Volatility), ensuring the AI doesn't recommend a confluence that only worked during a specific past market phase.
Suggested Text to Append to Your Spec
- **Weight Normalization:** All strategy condition weights must normalize to a total scale of 100%, allowing the live confluence score to be displayed intuitively as an aggregate percentage against the gate threshold.
- **Latency & Stale Data Guard:** If a live data source feeding a confluence condition is stale or disconnected at trigger time, the condition defaults to "Pending" and blocks entry execution.
- **No-Repaint Historical Rule:** All backtest and historical feature calculations must use strictly closed-bar data ($t-1$) to eliminate look-ahead bias.
- **Regime-Conditioned Confluence:** Per-condition contribution analytics must be segmented by market regime (Trending/Choppy) to avoid false edges tied to specific past market states.
