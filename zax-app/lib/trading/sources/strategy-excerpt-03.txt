1. Trend-Following (Swing / Position Trading)
Core Mechanics
Trend-following strategies operate on the premise that assets in motion tend to stay in motion. Rather than trying to predict a reversal at the bottom or top, trend traders wait for a directional trend to be confirmed and ride it until technical indicators signal market exhaustion.
 Timeframe: Daily (\bm{D}), 4-Hour (\bm{4H}), or Weekly (\bm{W}) charts.
 Holding Period: Days to several months.
How It Looks On Your Screen
 Indicators: Exponential Moving Averages (EMAs)—typically the 20-period and 50-period EMAs.
 Chart Setup: The price bar candles will sit persistently above moving averages during an uptrend. The moving averages themselves will fan out diagonally upward.
 Execution Trigger: A "Moving Average Pullback." You look for the asset price to temporarily pull back to touch the 20 EMA while the broader trend remains upward.

Key Execution Rules
1. Entry: Buy on the first green candle close that bounces off the 20 EMA following a minor pullback.
2. Stop Loss: Placed just below the lowest swing point or below the 50 EMA.
3. Exit/Take Profit: Use a trailing stop (e.g., trail 2 times the Average True Range, \bm{ATR}).

2. Breakout & Momentum Trading
Core Mechanics
Breakout strategies aim to capture fast, high-volatility price expansions that occur when price breaks through a key structural level (such as horizontal resistance, multi-month highs, or specific chart patterns like a Bull Flag).  
 Timeframe: 1-Minute, 5-Minute, or 15-Minute intraday charts.
 Holding Period: Minutes to hours (Day Trading).  
How It Looks On Your Screen
 Indicators/Tools: Level 2 Order Book, Time & Sales tape, Volume Profile, and Volume Relative (RVOL) scanners.
 Chart Setup: Price consolidates in a tight range or flag-like wedge (low volume), followed by a massive high-volume green candle breaking above resistance. Key Execution Rules
1. Filter/Scanner: Stock/Asset must have high relative volume (\bm{\text{RVOL} \ge 2.0\times} average) and a key fundamental or news catalyst.  
2. Entry: Place a buy-stop limit order 1 to 2 cents/ticks above the key resistance line or flag pattern high.
3. Stop Loss: Placed directly under the low of the consolidation pattern or the breakout candle.

Core Mechanics
Mean reversion strategies operate on the mathematical concept that asset prices, when stretched excessively far from their statistical average, eventually revert back toward that mean.
 Timeframe: 15-Minute, 1-Hour, or Daily charts.  
 Holding Period: Hours to days.
How It Looks On Your Screen
 Indicators: Bollinger Bands (20 period, 2 Standard Deviations) and Relative Strength Index (RSI - 14 period).  
 Chart Setup: The asset price pierces or stretches outside the outer upper or lower Bollinger Band while RSI moves into extreme territories (\bm{RSI > 70} for overbought, \bm{RSI < 30} for oversold).
